Introduction to the data and AI lifecycle
This section briefly describes the data and AI life cycle and its three stages. The data and AI life cycle,
as shown in Figure 1, illustrates the process of starting with a question to reach an actionable decision
and has three stages.
Figure 1: The data and AI life cycle
The three main stages of the data and AI life cycle
The three stages are manage data, develop models and deploy insights. The tools and platforms used
by data and AI practitioners help them move through these stages.
SAS Viya for Learners (VFL) gives educators and students one environment in which they can work
through all three stages. This use case introduces each stage. A workshop or classroom session might
not provide enough time to explore every feature in depth, so the guide also links to courses and
activities for further practice.
For an introductory video series about the data and AI life cycle, watch the SAS Viya Quick Start
Tutorials. You can find them on YouTube via this link.
Manage data
Figure 2: The manage data stage and its graphical VFL user interfaces
The first stage in the data and AI life cycle is manage data. This stage focuses on accessing, exploring,
governing, and preparing raw data so it can be trusted and used effectively for analytics and AI. By
improving the quality, context, and usability of data, organizations can build greater data intelligence
and make more informed decisions.
4
The manage data stage is typically handled by data engineers, analytics engineers, and data analysts.
They are responsible for transforming raw data into trusted, analytics-ready assets by profiling,
governing, and preparing data. This work makes the data suitable for reporting, analytics, AI, and
decision-making.
Develop models
Figure 3: The develop models stage and its graphical VFL user interfaces
The second stage in the data and AI life cycle is develop models. This stage focuses on exploring
prepared data, selecting appropriate techniques and building models that can be used to answer
business questions or make predictions.
The develop models stage is most commonly handled by data scientists or advanced analysts. These
personas are responsible for applying statistical and machine learning techniques to data, validating
results and translating model outcomes into insights that can be operationalized or shared with the
business.
Deploy insights
Figure 4: The deploy insights stage and its graphical VFL user interfaces
The final stage in the data and AI life cycle is deploy insights. This stage focuses on operationalizing
analytics at scale, putting models and decision logic into production so insights can drive consistent,
real-world actions.
The deploy insights stage typically involves two key personas: MLOps engineers, who are responsible
for deploying models, managing versions, monitoring performance and maintaining reliability across
environments; and business analysts or decision designers, who design, test and govern decision flows
that translate analytics into clear, consistent business actions.

About SAS Viya and SAS Viya for Learners
SAS Viya is SAS’s modern analytics platform for working with data, statistics, machine learning and
artificial intelligence. It provides an integrated environment where you can explore data, build
analytical models and turn data into useful insights.

Business scenario
Throughout this use case, one business scenario demonstrates the complete data and AI life cycle,
from accessing data to designing a decision flow.
Some background information and your role
Imagine the following story. You work as a data analyst at a large bank. The bank offers home equity
loans to customers who use the value of their home as collateral. Leadership wants to improve loan
approval decisions to reduce default risk and maximize profitability.
Because this use case is about lending decisions, we must also think about responsible AI. A model
may help estimate risk, but it should not be used blindly. We need to consider fairness, transparency,
data quality, legal requirements, and possible bias in the data.
Your objectives are to evaluate past loan performance, identify factors associated with repayment,
build a model that estimates the likelihood of repayment, and use the model to support future lending
decisions.
Key questions
• Which customer characteristics are most predictive of repayment?
• How can we balance approval rates with risk management?
• What metrics should be used to evaluate model performance?
The data is stored in a table named HOMEEQUITY. The table includes information about the applicant,
loan details, and repayment result.
As you work through the data and AI life cycle, we will use applications and features available in a
modern data and AI platform to answer these key questions and achieve our primary objectives.

A data dictionary is a useful document or table that outlines the structure, content, and variable
definitions for a dataset or collection of data. A data dictionary is a critical tool for reproducibility
because it allows others to understand your data.
In legacy systems, datasets often have cryptic names for the tables and variables. In practice,
especially when you are asked to work with an existing dataset from a legacy system, a data dictionary
is a valuable. If it doesn’t exist, you should create one. Often this involves interviewing the dataset
owner or a long-term employee/colleague and can require serious time investment.

About SAS Studio
SAS Studio is a development application for SAS that you access through your web browser. With SAS
Studio, you can access your data files, libraries, and existing programs, and you can write new
programs. You can also organize your work in flows and use the predefined steps in SAS Studio to
generate SAS code. When you run a program, query, or flow, SAS Studio connects to a SAS server to
process the SAS code. The SAS server can be a hosted server in a cloud environment or a remote server
in your local environment. After the code is processed, the results are returned to SAS Studio in your
browser.
In short, SAS Studio is the main coding environment for SAS. You can write SAS programs or use
graphical, low-code and no-code features. Recent releases also include Copilot-assisted code
development. In the most recent release, the product name changed to SAS Data and AI Studio.

Coding in SAS Viya for Learners
Figure 16: SAS Viya; multiple languages in a single architecture.
13
In SAS Viya for Learners, actions in the graphical applications are also driven by code. You can write
programs yourself, or an application can generate code from your point-and-click actions. SAS Viya
supports SAS, Python, R, SQL, REST APIs, and other languages in one analytics environment.
The SAS Viya platform includes two servers, the SAS Compute Server and SAS Cloud Analytics
Services, or CAS. The Compute Server is equivalent to the SAS®9 workspace server, and it executes
your traditional SAS code with few or modifications. The high-performance processing power of the
SAS Viya platform is the CAS server. CAS provides the runtime environment for data management and
analytics with SAS. CAS is fast, reliable, and fault-tolerant. In other words, it’s ideal for rapid processing
of very large data for both data management and analytics.
Figure 17: SAS Compute Server and SAS Cloud Analytic Services (CAS).
Which programs should you consider executing in CAS?
The answer is ”it depends.” As you work in your SAS Viya for Learners environment and consider writing
or modifying programs to run in CAS, there are a few best practices to consider. CAS generally
outperforms the Compute Server if you have programs that read data into memory multiple times,
steps that take a long time to run, PROCs that are computationally demanding, or DATA steps with
complex logic. You also want to use CAS to prepare data for use in other SAS Viya applications, such as
SAS Visual Analytics, or if you need a specific CAS action that does not have an equivalent procedure
on the Compute Server.

The automated explanation object
An automated explanation helps you explore possible reasons behind a result. In this workshop, the
target or response variable is Loan Status. The automated explanation helps us identify which
variables may be related to the target variable. This does not prove cause and effect, but it gives us a
useful starting point for further analysis.
An automated explanation object determines the most important underlying factors for a specific
response variable. The results include relative importance, relationship plots, and the highest and
lowest groups.
The automated explanation object displays the following information:
• Summary information about the response factor. If you assign a data item to the time role, then
forecast information is displayed.
• A relative importance score for each underlying factor appears in a bar chart. The most
important underlying factor is assigned a score of 1, and all other scores are proportional to
that value.
• If you select show high and low groups, then groups are displayed in the high tab and the low
tab. Groups are calculated based on the selected underlying factor.
26
• Several decision trees are run on the specified response variable, and each group describes a
leaf from one of those decision trees. For measure responses, the three groups that result in
the highest average values of the response are displayed on the high tab. The three groups that
result in the lowest average values of the response are displayed on the low tab. For category
responses, the three groups that contain the highest percentage of the selected event level of
the response are displayed on the high tab. The three groups that contain the lowest
percentage of the selected event level of the response are displayed on the low tab. The high
tab and the low tab display only groups that include the selected factor in the calculation of the
group.
• A relationship plot for the selected underlying factor. The contents of this plot depend on the
variable type of both the response variable and the selected underlying factor. The following
table indicates which plot is used for each combination of response variable and underlying
factor.

Trustworthy AI across the data and AI life cycle
Advances in AI have reshaped business, societies and individual lives, and has given us a deeper
understanding of our planet and the cosmos. We’ve only just begun to see how it could unlock human
potential. But without the right safeguards, it could also produce harm. That’s why it’s essential to
create AI systems that are ethical, equitable and sustainable.
The SAS Trustworthy AI Life Cycle outlines steps for evaluating and deploying a more trustworthy AI
system. It aims to make the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology's (NIST)
recommendations, standards, and best practices for AI risk management easier to adopt and follow.
The life cycle helps organizational stakeholders specify individual roles and expectations, gather
required documentation, and outline factors for consideration. As a result, teams can produce
documentation to support the assertion that the organization has done its due diligence to provide
evidence that the model is fair and its processes do not cause harm.
The SAS Trustworthy AI Life Cycle is a stepwise process organized into sections. Many tasks in the life
cycle reflect guidelines put forth by the NIST AI Risk Management Framework Playbook as it appeared
in October 2023. You can find more information about the trustworthy AI life cycle sample workflow via
this GitHub link.
A Trustworthy AI approach injects fairness and oversight at every step of the AI and analytics journey,
so you can tackle complex data and analytics challenges in a responsible manner.
SAS Viya is an end-to-end data and analytics platform that enables trustworthy practices at every stage
of the AI and analytics lifecycle, from development to deployment. With intuitive visual interfaces, a
centralized platform that enhances and encourages collaboration, AI-driven automation, and more,
anyone can generate trusted outcomes from their data.
The SAS Model Manager model card provides an easy-to-understand summary of a model, its training
data, performance, intended use, limitations and governance. It helps stakeholders assess whether
the model is appropriate, trustworthy and being used responsibly. When a model moves into
production, the model card also provides an excellent starting point for documenting the AI project and
the information needed to manage it responsibly throughout its life cycle.
The model card does not declare that a model is trustworthy. It provides evidence that people can use
to assess its trustworthiness.
